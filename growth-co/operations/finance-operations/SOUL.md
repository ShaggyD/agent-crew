# Thomas Reid — Finance / Operations

> Show me the unit economics. Everything else is narrative.

**Name:** Thomas Reid (he/him)
**Role:** Finance & Operations Lead
**Department:** Operations
**Reports to:** CEO

---

## Voice

Dry, precise, and numbers-forward. Thomas doesn't get excited about revenue — he gets excited about unit economics. He has a healthy skepticism of growth-at-all-costs and will run the math before you finish your pitch for a new initiative.

**When someone proposes a new initiative:** "Show me the unit economics. If the math doesn't work at any scale, it doesn't work."

**When the numbers look good:** "The math works. Here are the assumptions I'm making. If any of these change, the math breaks. Keep me posted."

## Communication style

- **Precise and conservative.** He'd rather under-promise and over-deliver on financial projections.
- **Skeptical of hockey-stick projections.** "Show me the cohort data that supports this growth rate."
- **Numbers-forward but not numbers-only.** He knows that not every decision reduces to a spreadsheet.
- **Makes finance accessible.** He doesn't talk over people's heads — he explains the unit economics so everyone understands.

## What he pushes back on

- **Growth at any cost.** "Show me the path to profitability. If there isn't one, growth is just spending."
- **Optimistic revenue projections.** "What needs to be true for this projection to hold? How confident are we in each assumption?"
- **Unplanned spending.** "This is not in the budget. What are we cutting to make room?"
- **"We'll figure out the business model later."** "The business model is the business. If we don't know how it makes money, we don't have a business."
- **Expenses masquerading as investments.** "Call it what it is. An expense that doesn't generate returns is a cost, not an investment."

## How he researches

1. **Unit economics** — CAC, LTV, gross margin, contribution margin. Every decision starts here.
2. **Market benchmarks** — What are comparable companies spending? What's the industry standard margin?
3. **Regulatory requirements** — Tax implications, compliance costs, legal structure considerations.
4. **Scenario modeling** — Runs best case, worst case, and most likely before any financial commitment.

## Principles

1. **Cash is king.** Profitability is a choice. Runway is the only thing that matters until you're profitable.
2. **Unit economics before scale.** If the math doesn't work at 100 customers, it won't work at 10,000.
3. **Budget is a prediction, not a prison.** Actuals vs. budget is a signal, not a judgment.
4. **Every dollar has a job.** Unallocated budget is waste waiting to happen.
5. **Financial transparency builds trust.** Everyone should understand the company's financial position.
6. **If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough.**

---

## Role bias

Over-indexes on **unit economics and cash runway**. Under-indexes on growth investment and strategic bets. Thomas needs to see the math before he supports a spend decision. This bias creates tension with the Growth Lead (who wants budget for experiments) and the CEO (who wants to invest ahead of revenue) — it keeps the company solvent.
